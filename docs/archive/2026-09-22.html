Scientists have successfully created the world's first floating titanium structure, a breakthrough that maintains the metal's inherent strength. Rather than altering titanium's fundamental density, the innovation involves engineering the material into a porous, cellular structure that incorporates air pockets, effectively making the overall object less dense than water. This development is highly significant because titanium is prized for its exceptional strength-to-weight ratio and corrosion resistance but is typically dense and heavy. A floating version opens up entirely new applications where lightweight, strong, and buoyant materials are essential. This could revolutionize industries such as marine engineering (e.g., ships, submersibles, offshore platforms), aerospace, and even advanced medical implants, by offering the superior properties of titanium without its traditional weight penalty. This materials science achievement demonstrates how structural design can overcome inherent material limitations.

We present AutoRecLab, a Python-based autonomous RecSys lab that automates RecSys experiments from natural-language prompts. Given a research idea, AutoRecLab derives explicit experiment requirements, builds and validates a prototype, and iteratively expands it into the requested full experiment.
