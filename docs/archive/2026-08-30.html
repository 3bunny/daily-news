Roketsan, a prominent Turkish defense company, recently conducted the inaugural ground-launch tests of its new ÇAKIR cruise missile. These tests successfully engaged both land and sea targets, marking a significant advancement in Turkey's coastal defense capabilities. This development matters because ground-launched cruise missiles offer a flexible and potent means of protecting a nation's maritime borders, strategic coastal assets, and shipping lanes. By demonstrating the ÇAKIR's ability to strike targets from land, Turkey enhances its deterrence posture against potential naval and land-based threats. The versatility of the ÇAKIR, designed to engage a range of surface targets, provides a multi-layered defense solution. Furthermore, as an indigenous product of Roketsan, it underscores Turkey's commitment to self-sufficiency in defense technology, reducing reliance on foreign suppliers and potentially opening avenues for defense exports. This is particularly relevant given Turkey's strategic location in the Black Sea and Mediterranean regions, where robust defense capabilities are crucial for national security. The ÇAKIR is a new generation cruise missile system, characterized by its ability to fly at low altitudes to evade radar detection and its modular design, allowing for deployment from various platforms including ships, aircraft, and now, ground vehicles. Cruise missiles are guided weapons that maintain a relatively constant speed and altitude during their flight, making them effective for precision strikes over long distances. The integration of ground-launched cruise missiles into coastal defense strategies represents a modern approach, moving beyond traditional artillery to sophisticated, long-range precision strike capabilities.

In this work, we presented, to the best of our knowledge, the first reported 12-CNOT decomposition of the double qubit excitation operator. We compared our new circuit with the previous SOTA 13-CNOT circuits in 4 different metrics. Our new circuit has the lowest CNOT count (12), lowest CNOT depth (8, roughly 27% reduction), and lowest total circuit depth (15, 25% reduction) among all the previous SOTA circuits. With output qubit relabeling, the CNOT depth can be further reduced to 7 (roughly 36% reduction from 11). Further, we only added 2 extra 1q gates (from 11 to 13) compared to the best of the SOTA circuits. As the double qubit excitation operator can be used as a building block hundreds or thousands of times in practical quantum algorithms, any reduction in such primitives compounds over the full circuit, resulting in significant overall resource savings.
