They trained a depth-recurrent model on combinations of rules and tested its ability to generalize to new puzzles, new combinations of known rules, rules never seen together, and entirely novel rule instances. The study compared two methods of providing rule information: symbolic descriptions versus solved examples. Attention Sinks in VLMs Are Borrowed, Not Seen

Visual attention sinks are image tokens that absorb a large share of attention over the image regardless of the query or task, and they mislead methods that treat attention maps …

Read more
This research investigates 'attention sinks' in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which are specific image tokens that absorb a disproportionately large share of attention, regardless of the query or task. These sinks can mislead interpretations of where a model is 'looking.' The study found that visual attention sinks are image tokens whose 'key' (a component in the attention mechanism) aligns with the key of the language model's 'text-sink.' This suggests that visual sinks 'borrow' attention from the text-sink rather than being inherently visually salient. Critically, modifying a sink's key to align with other image keys redirects its attention without altering the model's output. This finding is significant for VLM interpretability. Attention maps are often used to explain VLM decisions, but attention sinks can distort these maps, making it difficult to accurately understand model behavior. By identifying the mechanism behind these sinks and demonstrating that their attention can be redirected without impacting performance, this research provides crucial insights into VLM internal workings. This understanding can lead to more reliable interpretability tools and potentially improve VLM efficiency or robustness by mitigating these misleading artifacts, fostering greater trust and transparency in AI systems.
