How DARPA plans to get nuclear power in the size of a ‘AA battery’

★ StandoutMiniature nuclear powerBreaking DefenseJul 29, 21:00 KST

DARPA's Rads to Watts program is exploring ways to create miniature nuclear power cells, aiming for a size comparable to a AA battery. Seven teams are currently competing to develop these power cells, which are designed to last for years or decades without recharging.

Read more
DARPA's Rads to Watts program is funding seven teams to develop miniature nuclear power cells, aiming for a size comparable to a AA battery. These cells are designed to provide power for years or even decades without needing recharging. This initiative is significant because it seeks to revolutionize long-duration power sources. Current small batteries have limited lifespans, while existing nuclear power cells (radioisotope thermoelectric generators, or RTGs) are typically much larger and used for specialized applications like space probes. Successfully miniaturizing this technology could enable a new generation of autonomous systems, remote sensors, medical implants, and defense technologies that require sustained, maintenance-free power in challenging environments. The challenge lies in safely and efficiently harnessing the decay heat of radioisotopes in a compact form factor, distinct from nuclear fission reactors.
