Sci-MMR: Benchmarking Multi-Step Evidence-Grounded Scientific Reasoning in Multimodal Agents

★ Standoutcs.AI updates on arXiv.orgSep 12, 13:00 KST

arXiv:2609.11243v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Autonomous research agents are increasingly expected to search the literature, analyze experimental evidence, and generate scientific hypotheses. These capabilities require multi-step evidence grounded reasoning that progressively acquires, integrates, and verifies …

Read more
Researchers introduced Sci-MMR, a new benchmark designed to evaluate multimodal AI agents on multi-step, evidence-grounded scientific reasoning. Unlike existing benchmarks that often focus solely on final answer accuracy, Sci-MMR requires models to progressively acquire, integrate, and verify evidence from structured argument graphs, including scientific claims, citations, and visual evidence from figures. When testing frontier multimodal models, the study found that while models achieved reasonable answer accuracy, their ability to recover all supporting evidence was significantly lower, indicating a gap in true evidence-grounded reasoning. The research identifies bottlenecks in how models acquire and integrate evidence. This matters because for AI to genuinely assist in scientific discovery, it must not only provide correct answers but also demonstrate a verifiable, traceable reasoning process. Trust in AI-generated hypotheses or analyses hinges on understanding how conclusions are reached. Sci-MMR pushes AI development towards more transparent and robust scientific reasoning capabilities, moving beyond superficial pattern matching to foster AI agents that can truly contribute to scientific research with verifiable insights. The findings highlight that current models may not possess the deep understanding required for complex scientific tasks.

arXiv:2609.11542v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Large language model (LLM) training is among the fastest-growing sources of electricity demand in modern data centers, and power availability is a primary bottleneck to continued AI infrastructure growth. Making the power consumption of these workloads flexible could …

Read more
This paper introduces the first systematic characterization of "job power elasticity" in large language model (LLM) training. It investigates how the performance (throughput) of LLM training workloads changes when the power supplied to GPUs is intentionally reduced. The researchers developed a "Power Flexibility Index (PFI)" to quantify the performance cost associated with power reductions and collected extensive data from 131 LLM training runs on H200 GPUs, covering various model types and tasks. This research is significant because LLM training is a rapidly escalating source of electricity demand, straining data center power availability and grid infrastructure. Understanding power elasticity enables the development of "power-flexible" AI training, where workloads can dynamically adjust their power consumption. This flexibility could unlock additional capacity for AI growth, mitigate increases in electricity prices, and improve the utilization of existing power grids by allowing data centers to respond to real-time energy supply and demand fluctuations. It's a crucial step towards more sustainable and economically viable AI infrastructure development.

Probabilistic Focal Search: Accelerating Bounded-Suboptimal Search via Lower-Bound Advancement

cs.AI updates on arXiv.orgSep 12, 13:00 KST

arXiv:2609.10584v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Bounded-suboptimal search seeks a solution within a factor $w$ of optimal while reducing search effort. Focal Search (FS) uses heuristic guidance within FOCAL, the frontier nodes eligible under the threshold $w f_{\min}$, but its deterministic policy may leave …

Read more
Researchers introduced Probabilistic Focal Search (PFS), an improved algorithm for bounded-suboptimal search. Bounded-suboptimal search aims to find a solution within a guaranteed factor of the optimal solution, but with reduced computational effort compared to finding the absolute optimum. PFS enhances the existing Focal Search (FS) by introducing a probabilistic element: with a certain probability, it follows FS's heuristic guidance, but with a complementary probability, it expands a node that helps advance the "lower bound" of the optimal solution cost. This mechanism balances heuristic guidance with progress on the lower bound, which can enlarge the search frontier (FOCAL) more effectively. This work matters because search algorithms are fundamental to many AI applications, including planning, robotics, and game playing. Finding optimal solutions is often computationally prohibitive for complex problems. Bounded-suboptimal search offers a practical alternative, and PFS aims to make this process even more efficient. By accelerating the discovery of good-enough solutions, PFS can enable faster decision-making and problem-solving in scenarios where time is critical, making AI systems more responsive and applicable to real-world challenges with vast search spaces.
