Read more
Traditional financial giants like Goldman Sachs, Citi, Mastercard, and Visa are aggressively entering the "onchain dollar" space, a digital representation of fiat currency on a blockchain. This move, exemplified by initiatives like Goldman and Citi's "Project Faro," marks a significant shift from a landscape previously dominated by crypto-native stablecoins such as Tether and Circle's USDC. This development matters because it signals a profound integration of traditional finance with blockchain technology. By tokenizing fiat currencies, Wall Street aims to leverage the efficiency, transparency, and programmability of distributed ledgers for faster and cheaper settlements, potentially revolutionizing everything from cross-border payments to securities trading. It could also introduce a new era of regulated digital money, offering greater stability and consumer protection compared to some existing stablecoins, which have faced scrutiny over their reserves and regulatory compliance. The "onchain dollar" is essentially a digital version of a fiat currency, like the US dollar, issued on a blockchain. Unlike traditional bank deposits, these tokens can be programmed for specific uses and settled almost instantly, bypassing legacy financial infrastructure. This push by established institutions could legitimize and accelerate the adoption of digital assets in mainstream finance, creating a more interconnected and efficient global financial system.
