U.S. Navy Commissions Flight III Destroyer USS Ted Stevens (DDG 128) in Alaska

Naval commissioningNaval NewsOct 04, 16:35 KST

The U.S. Navy has officially commissioned the USS Ted Stevens (DDG 128), its newest Flight III Arleigh Burke-class guided-missile destroyer, in Whittier, Alaska. This advanced vessel is equipped with the SPY-6 radar and Aegis Baseline 10 combat system, representing the latest iteration of the Burke-class destroyers.

Read more
The U.S. Navy has commissioned the USS Ted Stevens (DDG 128), its newest Flight III Arleigh Burke-class guided-missile destroyer, marking its official entry into active service. This event is significant because the USS Ted Stevens represents a major leap in naval capability, primarily due to its integration of the advanced SPY-6 radar and Aegis Baseline 10 combat system. The Arleigh Burke-class destroyers are the backbone of the U.S. Navy's surface fleet, known for their multi-mission capabilities, including anti-air, anti-surface, and anti-submarine warfare. The "Flight III" designation signifies the latest and most capable variant, specifically designed to accommodate the powerful SPY-6 (AN/SPY-6(V)1) Air and Missile Defense Radar. This new radar provides vastly improved detection and tracking capabilities for ballistic missiles, cruise missiles, and aircraft simultaneously, significantly enhancing the ship's ability to defend against complex threats. The Aegis Baseline 10 combat system is the software and hardware suite that integrates the SPY-6 radar, allowing the ship to process and act on the advanced data it collects. This upgrade is crucial for maintaining the U.S. Navy's technological edge and its capacity for ballistic missile defense (BMD) in an evolving global threat landscape. Commissioning such an advanced warship reinforces the Navy's ability to project power, ensure maritime security, and protect national interests worldwide. The ship is named after Senator Ted Stevens, a distinguished Alaskan politician and World War II veteran.
