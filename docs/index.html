The U.S. Navy plans to equip its Zumwalt-class destroyer, USS Michael Monsoor (DDG-1001), with Conventional Prompt Strike (CPS) hypersonic missiles during a scheduled overhaul in 2028. This marks the third Zumwalt-class vessel slated for this significant upgrade, following USS Zumwalt (DDG-1000) in 2025 and USS Lyndon B. Johnson (DDG-1002) in 2026. This development is crucial for several reasons. Hypersonic missiles, capable of traveling at speeds exceeding Mach 5 and executing complex maneuvers, are extremely difficult to detect and intercept, offering a transformative strike capability. Integrating CPS into the Zumwalt class repurposes these stealthy, large-platform destroyers into formidable missile carriers, validating their strategic relevance despite initial challenges with their original gun systems. This move is part of a broader U.S. effort to regain a competitive edge in hypersonic weapon technology against rivals like China and Russia, providing the ability to strike high-value, time-sensitive targets globally with conventional warheads. The Zumwalt class, initially designed for land attack with advanced but ultimately costly gun systems, consists of three ships. Their unique stealth design and integrated power system make them suitable platforms for advanced weapon systems. The CPS program aims to deliver a rapid, long-range conventional strike capability, enhancing the U.S. military's deterrence and response options in a complex global security environment.

The Barracuda program aims to deliver six Suffren-class SSNs, with the first, 'Suffren', commissioned in 2020. These submarines incorporate cutting-edge technologies, including a new pump-jet propulsion system for quieter operation, advanced sonar suites, and the capacity to launch Naval Cruise Missiles (MdCN) for precision deep strikes against land targets.
