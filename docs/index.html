U.S. Debt Tops GDP for First Time Since 1946, Lifting Global Rates Seoul Economic Daily

Read more
The U.S. national debt has surpassed its Gross Domestic Product (GDP) for the first time since 1946, a development reported to be influencing global interest rates. This milestone signifies that the total amount of money the U.S. government owes is now greater than the value of all goods and services produced by the economy in a year. This high debt-to-GDP ratio raises concerns about the long-term fiscal sustainability of the U.S., including the government's capacity to manage its debt obligations without resorting to measures like increased taxation or reduced public spending. The connection to global interest rates stems from the U.S. Treasury market's role as a benchmark for international finance. When the U.S. government needs to borrow more, or if investors perceive higher risk, it can drive up demand for higher yields on U.S. bonds, which then ripples through global markets, increasing borrowing costs for other nations and businesses worldwide. The surge in debt was largely fueled by extensive government spending during the COVID-19 pandemic.
