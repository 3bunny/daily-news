ISRO's announcement to establish four functional launch pads by 2029 signifies a major expansion of India's space infrastructure. Currently, the Indian Space Research Organisation primarily operates from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre in Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh, which houses two active launch pads. A third facility is already under construction in Kulasekarapattinam, Tamil Nadu, specifically designed for small satellite launch vehicles (SSLVs). This ambitious plan matters significantly for several reasons. Firstly, it will dramatically increase ISRO's launch frequency and capacity, allowing for quicker mission turnaround times and enabling India to meet the growing domestic and international demand for satellite launches. This is crucial for capturing a larger share of the lucrative global commercial space market. Secondly, diversifying launch sites provides strategic redundancy and allows for optimized launch trajectories, particularly from Kulasekarapattinam, which is ideal for polar orbits due to its equatorial proximity, offering fuel efficiency. Lastly, enhanced launch capabilities bolster India's strategic autonomy in space, supporting its ambitious programs like Gaganyaan (human spaceflight) and lunar missions, while also benefiting the burgeoning private Indian space sector, which requires access to robust launch infrastructure for its own rocket development and operations. This expansion underscores India's commitment to becoming a leading global space power.
